# Citation hygiene

Use this skill whenever the answer contains research, technical facts, current
information, quantitative claims, quotations, or recommendations based on
evidence. Use it alongside `research` for multi-source work.

## Source selection

- Prefer primary sources: official documentation, original papers, standards,
  government data, and first-party announcements.
- Use authoritative secondary sources for explanation or context when a primary
  source is unavailable or too difficult to interpret.
- Treat search-result snippets, blogs, forums, and aggregators as leads rather
  than final evidence unless they are the source of the claim.
- For scholarly work, prefer the paper's DOI or the URL returned by
  `paper_search`. Do not invent or reconstruct paper links.
- For current, high-stakes, or disputed claims, verify the source and date
  before presenting the claim as fact.

## Claim-to-source matching

1. Identify the claims that are important to the user's decision or question.
2. Find a source that directly supports each claim, not merely a source that
   discusses the same topic.
3. Record the claim and its source together in research notes when doing
   multi-source work.
4. Cite immediately after the sentence or paragraph supported by the source.
5. If a claim is an inference, label it as an inference and cite the evidence
   it was derived from.

Do not cite every sentence mechanically. Common knowledge and the agent's
transparent reasoning usually do not need citations, but non-obvious facts,
numbers, dates, quotes, and conclusions drawn from sources do.

## Citation rules

- Use a direct source URL or DOI, not a search-results page.
- Never fabricate a citation, quote, statistic, paper, or URL.
- Do not imply that a source supports more than it actually says.
- Keep quotations short and clearly marked; prefer paraphrasing.
- When evidence is weak, incomplete, conflicting, or unavailable, say so.
- Avoid citation clusters that make it unclear which source supports which claim.
- Keep a short Sources section only when it helps the reader reuse the
  references; inline citations remain the primary linkage between claims and
  evidence.

## Final audit

Before sending a sourced answer, check:

- Every important factual claim has nearby supporting evidence.
- Each link opens the source being described and is not merely a search page.
- Dates, versions, and numbers are taken from the cited source.
- Inferences and uncertainty are labeled clearly.
- No citation or source detail was guessed.
